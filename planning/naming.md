# Naming: a replacement for "Liftnext"

Owner: brand naming (brought in by the coordinator). Written 2026-11-07 against live **v3.516** (`APP_NAME = 'Liftnext'`, `index.html:7622`). Inputs: `design-vision.md` (Plate & Press, the Sash), `design-directions.md` §5–§6 (the bib, "Your number", "Pin it on"), `marketing.md` §2.1 and §3.4, `alignment/market-researcher.md` B1, `alignment/marketing-manager.md` B1, `research.md` §11, `PRODUCT.md`.

**Nothing here is a legal clearance.** The App Store and domain checks were run directly. The trademark, Google Play and social checks are web searches only. "free" below means only that the registry's RDAP server answered 404 on 2026-11-07, not that a registrar will sell it to us.

---

## 0. The short answer

- **Pick: Platestep** ("PLATE-step"). It's the only candidate of 128 that has **both `platestep.app` and `platestep.com` unregistered** and also passes every other test (eight others had both domains free but failed on meaning or on a neighbouring app). It had zero hits on every check: App Store (with reversed and plural forms), developer names, Play (web search), trademark aggregators and the open web. It says the product: progress one plate at a time.
- **Runner-up: Plateward** ("PLATE-ward"): plates, onward. `.app` is free, `.com` was registered 2026-01-10, and it had zero hits elsewhere.
- **Also cleared: Ironward, Kilonaut, Ironclimb.** Each carries a named risk (§3).
- Why Liftnext has to go, briefly: four App Store fitness apps are called "NextLift" or "Next Lifting", one has our pitch, and a NEXTLIFT filing is pending (`research.md` §11).
- Renaming is cheap: one display constant plus one canvas string (§5).

## 1. The naming brief

**What it names.** A free, ad-free strength app that tells you exactly what to lift next and then logs it in one tap. Its programs are evidence-based, its progression is honest (you add weight when you've earned it, and it tells you when you're stalling), and it's strength-first.

**Personality.** A good coach handing you a loaded bar: plain, exact and calm, never hype. It's printed, not lit (Plate & Press): flat plate inks, Archivo numerals, a race bib with your number on it.

**The name must signal:**
1. **Lifting and strength**: iron, plates, the bar. With "chalk" gone, the name has to do some of the work the bib's bar does now (`marketing.md` §2.4, §3.4).
2. **Progression**: the next step, small earned increments, a number that only goes up.
3. **Honesty**: earned, measured, true. No "transform", no "beast", no shortcut words.

**Avoid:**
- The LIFT- and NEXT- families: the NextLift collision, LIFT, Inc. marks, and the "imperative" problem ("LIFTNEXT 165 LB").
- CHALK- and FORGE-, which are crowded.
- Any health or medical claim, or anything that sounds like a drug or a device.
- Pub readings of "bar".
- Cardio or step-count readings.
- Imperatives that read as an instruction on a bib.
- Gamer or bro register.

**Hard constraints:**
- 12 characters or fewer, so it shows untruncated as an iPhone home-screen label.
- It reads well in tracked capitals on the bib's event band, beside the Sash tile: `[Sash] NAME · STRENGTH & SIZE · WK 3`.
- It can be said and spelled correctly after hearing it once.
- It makes no health or medical claim.
- No live fitness app has the same or a confusingly similar name.
- An unregistered `.app` or `.com`, or a clean variant of one.
- No negative meaning in Spanish, French, German or Portuguese.

## 2. Method, and every candidate screened

**Checks run, all on 2026-11-07:**
- **App Store:** Apple's iTunes Search API, US store, `entity=software`, 25 results per query. For every candidate the queries were `NAME`, `NAME lift` and `NAME workout`, flagging any title that contains the name, letters only. For the finalists, the API was also queried with the words split, reversed, pluralised, misheard spellings and `+gym`/`+fit`, plus `attribute=softwareDeveloper`.
- **Domains:** the registries' own RDAP servers. For `.app`, Google Registry (`pubapi.registry.google/rdap`); for `.com`, Verisign (`rdap.verisign.com/com/v1`). Both were calibrated first: `google.app` and `liftnext.com` returned 200, and nonsense names returned 404. `.fit` went through rdap.org, calibrated with `gym.fit` and `run.fit` (200). **`.io` could not be checked**: rdap.org returned 404 even for `socket.io`, which is registered.
- **Google Play:** web search only, restricted to `play.google.com`. Play's own search wasn't reachable.
- **Trademarks:** web search across trademarks.justia.com, markinton, trademarkdir, gleanmark and uspto.report. **This is not a USPTO clearance.**
- **Meaning:** my own reading in Spanish, French, German and Portuguese. No native-speaker review.

**Routes:** coined (14), plate/iron/bar (13 + extras), progression (12), meet/platform (11), real words (12), compounds (≈60). **128 candidates screened.**

## 3. The shortlist (5)

All five passed: no App Store fitness or health app with the same or a confusingly similar name; an unregistered `.app` (and `get<name>.app`); no trademark hit in the web searches; 12 characters or fewer; no negative meaning found in ES, FR, DE or PT.

### 3.1 Platestep (recommended)
- **Say it:** "PLATE-step" /ˈpleɪt.stɛp/. Two common words, spelled as heard.
- **Story:** progress one plate at a time. Every session is the next step up the bar, and only when you've earned it.
- **On the bib:** `[Sash] PLATESTEP · STRENGTH & SIZE · WK 3`. It's a noun, so it reads as the event's name, not an instruction, and it can sit beside a load ("PLATESTEP · 160 LB" doesn't read as a command). The subtitle "Know what to lift next" still works under it.

**Risks:**
- "Step" can suggest step counts or step aerobics, so lead with the plate and the Sash.

- `.io` is unknown.

## 4. Recommendation

**Platestep.** Of 128 candidates it's the only one that clears everything with nothing left over:
- **Both** the `.app` and the `.com` are free. Each of the other four finalists has only the `.app`. The eight other names with both domains free each failed elsewhere: Kiloplate, Vantrep, Liftbib, Platebib, Heftlog, Hoistlog, Ladderload and Plateclimb (see §2).
- No app, developer, Play listing, trademark or web use of the word.
- It's two everyday words that anyone spells after hearing them once.

It also fits the world we've built:
- **The plate is the brand's whole visual system:** the Sash is five plates' inks, and the day's spot ink is the day's plate.
- **The step is the product's promise:** an honest, earned next increment, never a leap.

On the bib it's a noun, so it fixes Liftnext's "reads as an instruction" problem (`marketing.md` §2.1 name rules), and "Know what to lift next" can stay as the subtitle.

**Runner-up: Plateward.** It has the same story with more momentum. It loses only because its `.com` was taken in January 2026 and the "-ward" spelling is less certain on first hearing.

**If the owner wants the boldest-sounding name:** Ironward. Only after counsel weighs the game studio.

**Store title, if Platestep:** "Platestep: Training Log" (23 characters), keeping the descriptor as `marketing.md` §2.1 did.

## 5. What the owner must do before it's final

These are in order; each is needs-owner.
1. **Choose** Platestep, Plateward or another shortlisted name.
2. **Buy the domains the same day.** The screen found lifting `.app` names being registered within days of each other.
   - For Platestep: `platestep.app` and `platestep.com`, optionally `platestep.fit`, with WHOIS privacy.
   - Check `platestep.io` at a registrar if wanted; it wasn't verifiable here.
   - Forward with HTTPS 301 as `marketing.md` §2.1 step 2 says. **Don't** set a GitHub Pages custom domain on the app repo: an origin change strands everyone's localStorage and IndexedDB data.
3. **Run a USPTO knockout yourself, or have counsel run it** (tmsearch.uspto.gov):
   - the exact name, its split form ("PLATE STEP") and sound-alikes;
   - classes **9** (downloadable software), **41** (fitness instruction) and **42** (SaaS);
   - also EUIPO eSearch and the WIPO Global Brand Database;
   - record the result in this file.
   - For Ironward, this step needs counsel because of the studio.
4. **Search the App Store and Google Play on a phone.** The API and web search aren't the stores' own ranking.
5. **Grab the handles**, if wanted (Instagram, TikTok, X, Threads). Their availability wasn't verified here.
6. **Decide whether to file.** Class 9 first, before the name goes on share cards (MKT-03). This file is not legal advice.
7. **Then ship the rename as one version** (`APP_VERSION` and `sw.js` `VERSION` bumped together, with its own test). It's cheap:
   - `APP_NAME` at `index.html:7622` flows into the header, footer, reports, backup messages and the share title.

   - Plus the share-card canvas text at **`index.html:29608`, which still prints `'TRAINING LOG'`**, and the `training-log.png` filename (29623).
   - Storage keys, cache names and the backup format keep their `training-log` names.

8. **Update the docs** that still say Liftnext: `marketing.md` §2.1, §3.4 and §4.1, `pitch.md` D7, and backlog MKT-03, MKT-09 and MKT-16.
